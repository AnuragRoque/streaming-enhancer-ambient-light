Privacy Policy

QualityLock browser extension · Effective 26 October 2026

In short: QualityLock collects nothing.

  • No accounts, analytics, tracking, ads or crash reporting.
  • The extension never sends data to us or to anyone else. We don’t run any servers for it.
  • Everything it stores stays in your browser. The only exception is your settings, which your browser can sync through your own Google account.

What QualityLock does on the pages you visit

QualityLock improves video playback on websites. To do that it runs inside the pages you open, on every site, because a video can be on any page. It works entirely on your device:

  • Quality lock. When a streaming player downloads its list of video qualities (a DASH, HLS or Netflix manifest), QualityLock removes the low-quality entries before the player reads it. This happens in memory. The list is never saved or sent anywhere.
  • Video frames. Ambient light, blur fill, snapshots and timeline previews read the picture of the video you are watching to draw their effects. The frames are processed in the page and never leave your device. Browsers never let extensions read DRM-protected video, and QualityLock doesn’t try to.
  • Snapshots are saved as a file only when you click the snapshot button.
  • Timeline previews. For plain video files, the timeline’s hover previews may load the same video a second time, from the same website, to draw small frames. Nothing is sent anywhere else.

What websites can see

Because QualityLock runs inside web pages, the page you are on can technically notice it:

  • The website’s own scripts can read what QualityLock keeps in that website’s storage (above), and its display and toolbar settings while you are on the page. The list of sites where you turned it off is never given to any website.
  • When you use the Image backdrop, your picture is drawn inside the page you are watching, so that website’s scripts could read it while the Image mode is on. Don’t use a private photo.

None of this is sent anywhere by QualityLock. We mention it so you know what a website could see.

Permissions and why they are needed

Read and change data on all websites

The video toolbar, the backdrop and the quality lock have to run inside the page that plays the video, and that can be any site. The quality lock also has to start before the site’s player loads, so QualityLock can’t wait for you to click it first. It checks the page’s network responses only to recognise video quality lists, and it changes only the video and its player, plus whatever you ask for (like Lights off). It doesn’t keep or send what it sees, and it doesn’t record the pages you visit.

Storage

To save your settings and your backdrop picture in your browser.

Scripting

Picture-in-Picture and fullscreen only work in direct response to a click. When you press those buttons in the popup, QualityLock uses this permission to pass the click on to the page.

Sharing and selling

We don’t collect your data, so we have nothing to share or sell. QualityLock doesn’t use your data for advertising, credit decisions or anything unrelated to playing video. Its use of information follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your control

  • Turn it off on a site: in the popup, switch off On this site on the Now tab and reload. Nothing of QualityLock runs on that site after that.
  • Remove your picture: in the popup, go to Display → Black bar backdrop → Image → Remove.
  • Reset everything: Settings → Reset all settings. This also deletes the picture.
  • Remove all of it: uninstalling the extension deletes its settings and picture. Data kept in a website’s storage is removed when you clear that site’s data in your browser settings.

Children

QualityLock doesn’t collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If QualityLock ever starts handling data differently, this page will be updated first and the effective date above will change. The history of this page is public on GitHub.
